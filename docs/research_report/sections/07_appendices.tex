\section{Negative mechanism results}
\label{app:negative}

This appendix holds the measurements behind Section~\ref{sec:res-prototype}. They are
reported in full because the case for changing the loop's consultant rests on them, and
because each one closes a line of investigation that would otherwise look open.

\subsection{Conditions A, B and C}
\label{app:abc}

Three configurations of the loop were run at three seeds on both datasets, with the fold
partition fixed. Condition A is the canonical prototype-consultant loop. Condition B trains
the selector head whose entropy ranks edges and calibrates the consultant's temperature per
fold instead of pinning it. Condition C re-selects the two knobs on top of B. The graph
encoder and AGAF rungs are unaffected by any of the three and are shown for reference in
Table~\ref{tab:abc}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Pooled out-of-fold macro-F1, mean over three seeds, by condition.}
\label{tab:abc}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Dataset & Rung & A (canonical) & B (signals fixed) & C (B, re-selected) \\
\midrule
\multirow{3}{*}{UNSW}
 & loop          & 0.7644 & 0.7610 & 0.7809 \\
 & AGAF          & 0.7793 & 0.7793 & 0.7793 \\
 & graph encoder & 0.7437 & 0.7437 & 0.7437 \\
\midrule
\multirow{3}{*}{ToN}
 & loop          & 0.4521 & 0.4147 & 0.4630 \\
 & AGAF          & 0.4102 & 0.4102 & 0.4102 \\
 & graph encoder & 0.4336 & 0.4336 & 0.4336 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The decision rule was fixed before the runs, and it returns the same verdict in all three
conditions: the loop is not separated from AGAF on either dataset. On NF-UNSW-NB15 the
two-level interval for loop minus AGAF is $-0.0156$ [$-0.0552$, $+0.0268$] in A, $-0.0192$
[$-0.0594$, $+0.0197$] in B and $+0.0008$ [$-0.0464$, $+0.0447$] in C. On NF-ToN-IoT it is
$+0.0428$ [$-0.0316$, $+0.1105$], $+0.0060$ [$-0.0667$, $+0.0739$] and $+0.0527$ [$-0.0118$,
$+0.1194$]. Condition C carries the highest point estimate on both datasets. That is not a
separation, and its selection curves are flat, with NF-UNSW-NB15's argmax landing on the
boundary of the swept range at $k = 35$.

Both defects were genuinely removed in B, which is what makes the negative result
informative rather than a failed implementation. On NF-UNSW-NB15 the selector head's macro-F1
moved from the range 0.019 to 0.036 up to the range 0.646 to 0.684, mean disagreement fell
from 0.8975 to between 0.524 and 0.548, the consultant's maximum softmax probability rose
from 0.102 to 0.487, and the temperature fell from about 9.4 to 0.105. On NF-ToN-IoT the
selector head moved from 0.029 to 0.065 up to 0.293 to 0.333, disagreement fell from 0.8988
to between 0.580 and 0.638, maximum probability rose from 0.101 to 0.500, and temperature
fell from about 9.5 to 0.030. A better calibrated consultant and a genuinely
uncertainty-ranked selector made the loop worse on both datasets.

\subsection{What the canonical loop was actually injecting}
\label{app:magnitudes}

Table~\ref{tab:magnitudes} gives the mean absolute magnitude added to the 39-wide encoded

directly interpretable as a fraction of a standard deviation.

\label{tab:magnitudes}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}

Condition & NF-UNSW-NB15 & NF-ToN-IoT & \\
\midrule
A (canonical)        & 0.077 / 0.060 / 0.061 & 0.023 / 0.021 / 0.023 & \\
B (signals fixed)    & 1.228 / 1.171 / 1.218 & 2.796 / 2.854 / 2.762 & \\
C (B, re-selected)   & 1.103 / 1.100 / 1.158 & 2.845 / 2.893 / 2.992 & \\
\bottomrule

Under the canonical prototype configuration the mechanism was injecting between 0.02 and 0.08
of a standard deviation. Calibrating the temperature raised that by a factor of roughly 17 on
NF-UNSW-NB15 and roughly 125 on NF-ToN-IoT. Two things follow. The prototype loop's advantage
over feedback-off was late fusion rather than feedback, and the injection scale is close to
redundant with the learned bias strength, since condition C moved the scale from 2.0 to 5.0 and
from 20.0 to 10.0 while the end-to-end magnitude barely moved.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Environment.} Python 3.14.3 in a virtual environment built from
\texttt{requirements.txt}. All runs export \texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1}. Without that pin,
pooled macro-F1 drifts by roughly 0.012 at a fixed seed, which is larger than several of the
differences this report measures, and one earlier finding in the project was retracted because
of it.

\paragraph{Seeds and folds.} Three training seeds, 42, 1 and 2. The stratified five-fold
partition is generated once at seed 42 and held fixed across all three, so the pooled
out-of-fold edge set is identical between seeds and the seed-matched bootstrap is a paired
comparison. Fold-partition variance is therefore not measured anywhere in this report.

\paragraph{Pipeline.} Graph construction runs from
\texttt{src.pipeline.step1.run\_step1}, writing \texttt{pyg\_data.pt} and
\texttt{aggregated\_edges.csv} under \texttt{data/<dataset>/processed/step1/}. The downstream
stages run through one entry point:

\begin{quote}
\texttt{python -m src.pipeline.common.run\_pipeline --dataset <key> \textbackslash} \\
\texttt{\ \ --only fusion oof prototypes top\_k\_sweep feedback ladder}
\end{quote}

with \texttt{<key>} either \texttt{unsw\_nb15} or \texttt{ton\_iot}. The loop consults
the trained head when \texttt{train\_feedback} is given \texttt{--use-llm-head}. Without
that flag it reproduces the superseded prototype-consultant ladder, at the knobs kept under
\texttt{prototype\_superseded} in each dataset's selected-feedback config. Out-of-fold semantic embeddings must be
regenerated with
\texttt{step3.build\_oof\_gnn\_embeddings} before the fusion stage whenever the graph
encoder changes. The fusion stage does not do it, and will otherwise train on stale
embeddings.

\paragraph{Checks.} The repository's test suite pins the contracts against each other and
against the on-disk graphs, including a test asserting that both datasets declare the same
architecture. It runs with \texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1 python -m pytest tests/ -q} and reports
167 passing tests with 359 passing subtests. Two further scripts guard the manuscript itself:
\texttt{scripts/check\_report\_numbers.py} extracts every numeric token from the section
sources and checks it against the contracts, the archive, the dataset statistics and an
explicit allowlist, and \texttt{scripts/prose\_stats.py} measures the writing against the
standard used in the final editing pass. Both run from \texttt{docs/research\_report/build.sh}.

\paragraph{What is not reproducible from this repository.} The raw NF-ToN-IoT and NF-UNSW-NB15
CSV sources are not committed, being 532\,MB and comparable in size. The graphs built from them
are, so every result here can be re-derived without re-downloading the sources.
