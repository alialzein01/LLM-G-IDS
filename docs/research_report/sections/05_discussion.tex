\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:disc-rqs}

\paragraph{RQ1.} Measured on their own, the two branches are close on NF-UNSW-NB15 and far
apart on NF-ToN-IoT. The graph encoder reaches 0.7437 and 0.4336, the semantic rung 0.7353 and
0.2785. Read at face value that suggests the semantic view is nearly worthless under heavy
imbalance, and the consultant experiment shows that reading is wrong. Holding the embeddings,
the graph, the folds and the seeds fixed and changing only the classifier placed on those
embeddings lifts the loop by $+0.0697$ and $+0.0523$. The semantic branch's weakness as a rung
is therefore a property of the whitened prototype scorer rather than of the encoder underneath
it. The answer to RQ1 is that neither modality is inherently weaker, and that the read-out
chosen over the semantic embeddings matters more than the choice between the two views.

\paragraph{RQ2.} No. AGAF is not separated from the graph encoder on either dataset:
$+0.0362$ [$-0.0096$, $+0.0822$] on NF-UNSW-NB15 and $-0.0238$ [$-0.0921$, $+0.0607$] on
NF-ToN-IoT. The point estimates even disagree in sign. The NF-UNSW-NB15 difference is separated under the
seed-matched procedure, which is worth stating rather than hiding. It locates the uncertainty
precisely. At a fixed pair of training seeds the fusion stage looks like an improvement, and once
training-seed variance is admitted it stops looking like one. Under the rule fixed before the
runs, gated attention fusion does not deliver a separated gain over the structural branch it
fuses.

\paragraph{RQ3.} The answer splits, and the split is the finding. With the mechanism as a whole,
feedback adds something: real advice is above \texttt{head\_only} at every seed on both datasets,
with every interval excluding zero. With the mechanism isolated, by disabling output fusion so
advice can only arrive through the injection path, it adds nothing that survives a bootstrap. The
embedding-only consultant is at $-0.0046$ and $+0.0099$ against control, and the trained head is
at $+0.0007$ and $-0.0378$, the last of those a separated regression. The ladder-level ablation
therefore measures fusion and injection together, and the isolation experiment shows which of the
two is carrying it. The loop's advantage over feedback-off is consistent with semantic evidence
being available at the classifier at all, not with the re-entry path being what converts it.

\paragraph{RQ4.} Four factors were measured and three of them bind. Topology decides whether the
attention variant can work at all: 58.7\% of NF-UNSW-NB15 edges share an address pair, and
62.8\% of NF-ToN-IoT edges have a destination of in-degree one, and in both regimes the injected
bias cannot change a prediction. Repeated endpoint pairs also set a ceiling for any architecture
that classifies edges from their endpoints alone, which is why E-GraphSAGE scores 0.1194 where
385 of 656 edges are indistinguishable and 0.4141 where only 167 of 2{,}127 are. Class imbalance
decides whether fusion survives, as discussed below. Consultant strength is the fourth factor,
and it is the one the mechanism turned out to depend on entirely.

\paragraph{RQ5.} Against published architectures re-trained on the same rows, folds and metric,
the loop with its trained-head consultant is separated above every faithful configuration on
both datasets. With the prototype consultant it is not separated from E-GraphSAGE on
NF-ToN-IoT. The other rungs are weaker still: on NF-ToN-IoT neither the graph encoder nor AGAF is separated from
E-GraphSAGE as published, and on NF-UNSW-NB15 neither the graph encoder nor the semantic rung is
separated from TE-G-SAGE carrying our node features. How much of the margin is architectural is
answered by the decomposition. Of the gap between TE-G-SAGE as published and our loop rung on
NF-UNSW-NB15, roughly 45\% is attributable to tuning the graph scale and schedule, roughly 30\%
to our node features, and roughly 25\% to the architecture. Measured to AGAF instead the
architectural share is 15.1\%. A large headline margin against a published number is mostly a
statement about the representation both models were given.

\subsection{Imbalance is the axis the two datasets differ on}
\label{sec:disc-imbalance}

The two graphs were chosen to differ by roughly a factor of 47 in most-to-least-frequent class
ratio, drawn in Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance}, and that difference is where the ladder changes
shape. AGAF is the stage that does not
survive the change. It sits above the graph encoder on NF-UNSW-NB15 and below it on NF-ToN-IoT. The per-class table
shows the damage concentrated in classes the graph encoder already handles well:
\texttt{mitm} falls from 0.8838 to 0.7082 and \texttt{xss} from 0.3728 to 0.0844. A gate that
learns per-edge modality weights has to learn them from the same scarce rare-class edges it is
meant to help. On a graph where seven attack classes hold 35 edges or fewer, there is little to
learn from. AGAF is not uniformly harmed: it is the only rung that lifts
\texttt{password} from 0.0401 to 0.2950. The stage trades one set of rare classes for another
rather than failing outright.

The loop does not show that trade, under either consultant. It stays above the graph encoder on
both datasets and keeps its position when the imbalance regime changes. Comparing shapes rather
than levels, which is the only comparison the differing class counts permit, the property of
collapsing under imbalance belongs to the fusion gate rather than to the use of semantic
evidence as such.

\subsection{Where the loop's gain comes from}
\label{sec:disc-echo}

The loop is separated above the three other rungs, and the isolation experiment says the
separation is not produced by the part of the design the work set out to test. Four
observations explain that, and they point the same way.

First, the loop reaches the classifier by two routes and only one of them is the feedback
mechanism. Output fusion places the consultant's logits into the final classifier directly. The
injection path modifies the edge representations the next graph pass consumes. Disabling the
first and measuring the second is what Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} does, and the second
contributes nothing separated from zero with either consultant.

Second, only advice that is always right converts. The oracle, handed the true label through
the same learned projection every other arm uses, gains $+0.0369$ and $+0.1354$, both separated.
Its advantage is not the format of the vector it presents, because handing a realistic
consultant that exact format made things worse, and made the injected magnitude fall from
0.0767 to 0.0183 on NF-UNSW-NB15. The projection is learned, so it responds to a saturated
input by injecting less.

Third, the loop does not learn a trust it could calibrate. Replacing in-fold advice with
cross-fitted advice, which is honestly less reliable, moved the learned trust scalar by at most
0.0205 across four settings, and moved it the wrong way in one. What moved instead was the
injected magnitude, from 1.3430 to 1.1752 and from 1.3291 to 1.0745. The parameter that is
supposed to represent how much the loop believes its consultant is not doing that job.

Fourth, five treatments failed from five different directions. Supervising the selector head,
calibrating the consultant's temperature, weighting advice by measured per-class reliability,
saturating the advice format, and matching the advice's train-time and test-time reliability
were each tried and each reverted. Selection, weighting, format and reliability matching are
the obvious levers, and none of them is the binding one. What did work was changing the
consultant itself, which is the sixth attempt and the only successful one.

One diagnostic from the earlier configuration sharpens the point. Under the prototype
consultant the magnitude actually injected into unit-variance features was 0.077 and 0.023. The
mechanism was, in effect, switched off, and the ladder measured at the time was measuring
fusion while describing feedback. That is why this report separates the two paths everywhere
and why the isolation experiment, not the ladder, is what answers RQ3.

\subsection{What the configuration buys, and what it does not}
\label{sec:disc-contribution}

Two statements have to be held together. The trained-head configuration produces the first
ladder steps this project has measured that survive training-seed variance. It produces them on
two datasets, under one architecture, with the same folds and the same seeds. The loop is
separated above AGAF, above the graph encoder and above the semantic rung, and above every
faithful re-trained baseline. Under the prototype consultant none of those comparisons is
separated on either dataset, so the consultant is what turned an ordering of means into a
result.

What the configuration does not buy is a separated gain from the feedback mechanism itself.
With output fusion disabled, so that advice can reach the classifier only by re-entering the
edge representations, neither consultant is separated from doing nothing, and on NF-ToN-IoT the
stronger one is separated in the wrong direction. The system works. The part of it this work
set out to test does not carry the result.

The contribution is therefore instantiation plus measurement, stated narrowly. DAS reports
consistent gains from a closed feedback loop between a frozen graph model and a language model,
at node level on text-rich graphs. This work instantiates the same shape of mechanism at edge
level on network-flow graphs, with a deliberately constrained consultant that sees one edge's
text and nothing else, and finds that the refinement result does not transfer. It then isolates
why, which is the part that generalises: the channel has demonstrable capacity, an oracle
converts it on both datasets, and no consultant we were able to build does. LOGIN, GLANCE and
RoGRAD each establish a component of the design, uncertainty-driven consultation, learned
routing and iterative refinement respectively, and none of them is claimed here as novel. What
is new is the measurement, in a setting where the answer turns out to be negative.

\subsection{Limitations, read as findings}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

Seven limitations affect how the numbers should be read, and most of them are results in their
own right.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Edge identity depends on the label.} Flows are aggregated by source IP,
    destination IP and attack class, so ground truth participates in defining what an edge is.
    Every number in this report is a controlled comparison between representations and
    mechanisms, and none is a deployment-valid detection estimate. This is the limitation that
    most restricts what the work can claim.

    \item \textbf{Only training-seed variance is measured.} The fold partition is fixed at the
    seed-42 split, so every reported standard deviation and every interval is a lower bound on
    total uncertainty. Given that the two comparisons this report calls not separated are already
    close to zero, measuring partition variance could only widen them.

    \item \textbf{The loop trains on advice more reliable than the advice it is scored on.} Each
    fold's head has fitted that fold's training labels, so on training edges its logits are
    in-fold. Cross-fitting removes the asymmetry and makes the loop worse on both datasets, which
    means the effect is not a simple optimism bias that honest advice would fix.

    \item \textbf{Neither knob is tuned.} Both selection curves lie inside their own noise band,
    and NF-ToN-IoT's injection scale selected on the upper boundary of the swept range, so the
    sweep cannot say whether an optimum lies outside it. The word used throughout is selected.

    \item \textbf{One consultant file sits inside the loop at all three seeds.} The trained head
    was built once, at seed 42, and the loop consults that same file at every training seed. Its
    advice is therefore identical across the three runs. The loop's reported standard deviation,
    0.0042 and 0.0080, excludes consultant variance entirely and is narrower than a fully
    re-seeded pipeline would give.

    \item \textbf{The isolation experiment's pairs are not independent.} Its intervals are
    computed over fifteen (seed, fold) pairs, and the five folds within a seed share a training
    run. The effective sample size is smaller than fifteen, so those intervals are optimistic in
    the same direction for every arm.

    \item \textbf{E-GraphSAGE is within noise of our structural rungs on NF-ToN-IoT.} Neither the
    graph encoder nor AGAF is separated from it there. Only the two rungs that use a trained
    semantic consultant clear it, and only at the second consultant. The honest summary of RQ5 is
    that our structural pipeline matches a published architecture on that graph rather than
    improving on it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What follows from this}
\label{sec:disc-future}

Three directions follow directly, and one non-direction is worth recording.

Trust is the parameter to work on next. The loop has a scalar meant to represent how much to
believe the consultant, and it does not respond to a large, deliberate change in the
consultant's reliability. Constraining that scalar, supervising it against measured per-edge
reliability, or replacing it with a per-edge quantity are all reachable experiments, and the
oracle result says there is something to win if the consultant can be made trustworthy where it
is confident.

Deployment-valid graph construction is the prerequisite for any operational claim. It requires
an aggregation key that does not read the label, which changes the edge set, the class
distribution and the difficulty of the task all at once. That is a new set of experiments rather
than a re-run of these.

A stronger consultant is a reasonable third direction, with one caveat this work supplies:
standalone accuracy is not the property that matters. Through the injection path on
NF-ToN-IoT the stronger consultant is the worse one, at $-0.0378$ against control where the
weaker one sits at $+0.0099$. Calibration on the flagged subset, not accuracy over the whole
graph, is what a replacement consultant would have to improve.

The non-direction is advice that depends on the graph model's current belief. The consultant here
sees one edge's text and nothing else, which is what makes the coupling bidirectional in routing
and not in content. Conditioning the advice on the graph model's top candidates would very likely
change the result, and it was excluded by design so that the semantic branch's contribution could
be attributed to it alone. A future system that removes that constraint is testing a different
question, and it should say so.
