\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section first answers the research questions and compares the patterns observed on the two datasets. It then examines what the consultant and feedback mechanism contribute, states the limitations, and finally identifies the experiments needed to resolve the remaining questions.

\subsection{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:disc-rqs}

\paragraph{RQ1.} The GNN and prototype semantic model are close on NF-UNSW-NB15, at 0.7437 and 0.7353, but farther apart on NF-ToN-IoT, at 0.4336 and 0.2785. This does not establish that the semantic embeddings contain little useful information. A trained head on the same frozen embeddings reaches 0.8374 and 0.5081. The choice of classifier therefore matters when judging the semantic branch. Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports the head separately from the four-stage ladder.

\paragraph{RQ2.} Fusion does not establish a gain over the GNN on either dataset. The two-level differences are $+0.0370$ $[-0.0212, +0.0929]$ on NF-UNSW-NB15 and $-0.0235$ $[-0.0910, +0.0602]$ on NF-ToN-IoT. The NF-UNSW-NB15 difference is separated under the secondary seed-matched procedure, but not when the primary procedure includes independent training-seed variation. Under the decision rule fixed before the runs, the answer is negative on both datasets.

\paragraph{RQ3.} The complete trained-head feedback model improves over the graph-only control and the fusion stage, but not over the trained semantic head alone by a separated margin. With output fusion disabled, the prototype's differences against control are $-0.0046$ and $+0.0099$, neither separated. The trained head gives $+0.0007$ on NF-UNSW-NB15, also not separated, and a separated loss of $-0.0378$ on NF-ToN-IoT. This isolation experiment used earlier consultation percentiles, so it does not directly quantify injection at the final settings. Together, the experiments show the value of semantic predictions in the complete model but leave an additional injection benefit unestablished.

\paragraph{RQ4.} Model behaviour changes across the two graphs, but the cause cannot be assigned to imbalance alone. The graphs also differ in size, density and repeated endpoint pairs. Topology constrains attention injection: parallel edges share endpoint representations, while an incoming neighbourhood of one makes its attention weight fixed. Consultant choice also matters, as the prototype and trained head behave differently on the uncertain edges. The per-class results show where fusion gains and loses; they do not isolate why those changes occur.

\paragraph{RQ5.} Trained-head feedback is separated above the faithful re-trained baselines on both datasets, while its difference from the head alone is not separated. These are comparisons on our aggregated representation, not against published benchmark scores. On NF-ToN-IoT, the GNN and fusion stages are not separated from E-GraphSAGE with its published settings. On NF-UNSW-NB15, the point-estimate decomposition associates roughly 45\% of the gap from TE-G-SAGE to feedback with validation selection, 30\% with node features and 25\% with the remaining model change. Those increments have no confidence intervals and should not be read as a general architectural ranking.

\subsection{Patterns across the two imbalance regimes}
\label{sec:disc-imbalance}

The datasets were selected to represent different class distributions. Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance} shows the graph-level imbalance and distinguishes it from imbalance among the evaluated classes. Fusion has a higher mean macro-F1 than the GNN on NF-UNSW-NB15 and a lower mean on NF-ToN-IoT. Neither difference is separated under the primary bootstrap. The comparison establishes this change in ordering, but two datasets cannot isolate imbalance from their other differences.

The NF-ToN-IoT class results show a trade-off. Fusion raises \texttt{password} from the GNN's 0.0401 to 0.2996, while \texttt{mitm} falls from 0.8838 to 0.7082 and \texttt{xss} from 0.3728 to 0.0844. Explaining the net loss would require further controlled tests. We therefore do not attribute it to a particular gate behaviour or to imbalance alone. Small class counts also make these point estimates sensitive to a few predictions.

Macro-F1 and class-weighted focal loss address the imbalance in different ways: the metric gives each evaluated class equal importance, while the loss changes the training emphasis. Additional oversampling was measured and was harmful when combined with the existing class weights, so it is disabled. These choices do not eliminate the uncertainty associated with rare classes.

The prototype's low NF-ToN-IoT score is also insufficient evidence of an encoder limitation. The trained head performs better on the same frozen embeddings, showing that the readout affects the result. However, no experiment adapted CySecBERT to the templated flow descriptions. The effects of the readout, data imbalance and encoder adaptation have therefore not been fully separated.

\subsection{Where the feedback model's gain comes from}
\label{sec:disc-echo}

The feedback model has two semantic routes. Injection changes the edge representation before another graph pass. Output fusion gives the final classifier direct access to the semantic predictions. A gain from the complete model cannot, by itself, tell us which route helps. The trained head alone has a slightly higher mean macro-F1 than feedback on both datasets, with no separated difference between them.

The isolation diagnostic in Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} disables output fusion. An oracle with access to the true label improves through edge injection on both graphs, demonstrating that this route can change accuracy under the tested conditions. Neither realistic consultant produces a separated improvement there. The trained head even produces a separated loss on NF-ToN-IoT. This is evidence that a stronger standalone classifier need not supply better advice for injection.

The prototype treatments offer further diagnostic evidence. Changing selection, temperature, reliability weighting, advice format and cross-fitting did not establish the intended improvement. In the format experiment, larger input logits led to smaller injected magnitudes. Cross-fitting changed injected magnitudes more than the learned trust scalar. Appendix~\ref{app:negative} records these measurements and their single-seed or multi-seed scope. They motivate investigating how advice is used, without proving that every alternative mechanism would fail.

The most direct conclusion is that the complete system benefits from strong semantic predictions, while the experiments do not establish a further gain from injection. Because the isolation diagnostic used earlier consultation percentiles, a matched diagnostic at the final settings would be needed to attribute the final system's performance more precisely. The head-alone comparison remains the direct check on whether the full system adds measurable value beyond its consultant.

\subsection{What the configuration buys, and what it does not}
\label{sec:disc-contribution}

The trained-head configuration gives separated improvements over the GNN, semantic and fusion stages and the faithful re-trained baselines on both datasets. It does not establish an improvement over the trained head alone. This distinction explains why the report includes both a four-stage architectural comparison and a separate component comparison. Omitting the consultant's standalone result would make the feedback stage appear to add more than the measurements support.

The choice of trained head is supported by its stronger standalone predictions and its higher accuracy on the consulted edges. Earlier prototype treatments failed to establish the intended gain, which motivated this change. However, the consultant and consultation percentile changed together, and the difference between the two feedback configurations is separated only on NF-UNSW-NB15 under the primary procedure. We can justify the choice without claiming that the experiments isolate its causal effect or establish a feedback gain over its own component.

The contribution is the implementation and evaluation of an edge-level structural--semantic system, including tests that distinguish a useful consultant from a useful injection mechanism. The underlying ideas of graph--language feedback, uncertainty-based consultation and learned routing come from the prior work reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:related}. The measurements here establish both positive comparisons and limits on what can be credited to the feedback mechanism.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

The following limitations determine how broadly the results can be interpreted.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Controlled graph construction.} The deployment restriction and the changes needed to address it are developed in Section~\ref{sec:method-graph}. The present results must be interpreted within that scope.

    \item \textbf{A fixed fold partition and small classes.} Training seeds vary, but the fold partition does not. The reported uncertainty therefore excludes partition variation. Several evaluated classes contain only 12--40 edges, and no per-class difference has a confidence interval.

    \item \textbf{Training advice differs from test advice.} Each fold's semantic head has seen its training-edge labels, so its advice on those edges is in-fold. The reported cross-fitting diagnostics do not establish a reliable remedy. Their settings and evidence are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:negative}.

    \item \textbf{Flat selection curves and a boundary value.} The feedback settings are selected from curves that are flat relative to training variation. NF-ToN-IoT's scale is at the upper search boundary. Performance outside the tested range is unknown.

    \item \textbf{Fixed consultant advice across feedback seeds.} Feedback training reuses the seed-42 head predictions. Its reported standard deviation excludes the variation that would arise from retraining its consultant. A fully re-seeded pipeline was not measured.

    \item \textbf{Scope of the isolation experiment.} Its consultation percentiles differ from the final configuration. Its paired intervals also use fifteen (seed, fold) pairs, which are not independent because their training sets overlap. These diagnostics should not be treated as a matched decomposition of the final model.

    \item \textbf{Baseline representation and scoring differences.} E-GraphSAGE is not separated from our GNN or fusion stage on NF-ToN-IoT. The reported fusion and baseline runs also allowed predictions of excluded classes. Appendix~\ref{app:baseline-detail} bounds the possible cost; the code was corrected without replacing the reported measurements.

    \item \textbf{An unadapted semantic encoder.} CySecBERT remains frozen, and no fine-tuned or domain-adapted version was evaluated on the flow templates. The current results cannot determine what such adaptation would add.

    \item \textbf{Unisolated structural contributions.} No run disables message passing, no flat classifier is evaluated on the same edge and centrality features, and no ablation compares the three weighted branches with one unweighted branch. The value of these structural design choices is therefore not established separately.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What follows from this}
\label{sec:disc-future}

The first experimental priority is a matched comparison of the trained head alone, output fusion without injection, and the complete feedback model at the final consultation settings. It should vary the consultant's training seed as well as the feedback model's seed. This would directly test whether the added mechanism contributes beyond access to strong semantic predictions.

A second direction is the reliability of advice on the edges selected for consultation. The oracle diagnostic shows that correct advice can help through the channel, but the trained-head diagnostic shows that stronger overall classification need not improve injection. A per-edge reliability estimate or a supervised trust function could be tested against the existing scalar, with all comparisons using the same settings and controls.

Deployment-valid evaluation requires the graph and temporal protocol changes described in Section~\ref{sec:method-graph}. It is a separate experiment on a changed task. Advice conditioned on the graph model's current predictions would also change the question: the present consultant reads only an edge's text, and its advice does not change when the graph model changes its belief.

\subsection{Scope, and the experiments this work did not reach}
\label{sec:disc-scope}

The remaining experiments include repeating the evaluation with new fold partitions, extending the boundary-selected scale range, and adapting the frozen encoder to the flow descriptions. None was run for the results reported here. Their absence is a limit on the evidence, rather than a reason to assume that they would improve the scores.

The structural branch needs its own controls. Disabling message passing and fitting a flat classifier to the same aggregated edge attributes and endpoint centralities would test whether graph propagation adds useful information. Comparing the three weighted encoders with a single unweighted encoder would test the branch split. These comparisons address the supervisor's open methodological questions and remain future work.
